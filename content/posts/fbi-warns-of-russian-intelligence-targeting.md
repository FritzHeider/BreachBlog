**Russian Intelligence Services (RIS) are executing a sophisticated and persistent social engineering campaign designed to steal Signal Backup Recovery Keys from high-value targets, fundamentally compromising end-to-end encrypted communications.** This insidious operation, tracked by the intelligence community as UNC5792 and UNC4221, represents a deliberate escalation in digital espionage, moving beyond account hijacking to direct data exfiltration from encrypted backups. The FBI and CISA have issued urgent warnings, including advisories in March and June 2026, detailing how officers embedded with Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) Border Guards and other proxy actors are leveraging deceptive in-app messages to coerce victims into divulging their unique 64-character recovery keys. These attacks exploit human trust, not a technical flaw in Signal's robust encryption, meaning no CVE numbers are associated with this social engineering vulnerability. However, the operational impact is indistinguishable from a catastrophic software exploit. Mandiant researchers have extensively documented the tactics employed by UNC5792, revealing a pattern of targeting governmental and military personnel. The shift from SMS code theft to direct recovery key solicitation signifies a more efficient and devastating attack vector, as a stolen key grants access to an entire history of encrypted messages. This campaign is a chilling reminder that even the strongest cryptographic protections are only as secure as their weakest human link. If you were the CISO here, you would be seeing a surge of internal reports from users questioning unusual Signal messages, a clear indicator of a widespread, targeted campaign. Organizations that fail to proactively educate and protect their high-risk personnel against this specific threat are choosing convenience over the integrity of sensitive communications. The irony of using a secure messaging app only to have its backups compromised by a simple trick is not lost on those who understand the delicate balance of technology and human behavior. Note what this means: The entire security model of encrypted communication platforms like Signal hinges on the absolute secrecy of private keys and recovery mechanisms. When state-sponsored actors can bypass cryptographic strength through psychological manipulation, it exposes a systemic failure in user education and organizational security awareness. This shifts the attack surface from complex protocol vulnerabilities to the inherent human propensity for trust and urgency, rendering even the most advanced encryption moot against a determined, well-resourced adversary. Why does this keep happening?
